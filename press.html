Cricket,
with character.

Bats, spin, safe paws — and time for tea.

Cricket Kennel reimagines the game as a gathering of original dog characters. Its first release brings together 20 illustrated teammates, pairing expressive breeds and cricket detail with sourced tributes to the players who inspired them.

From Brian Labra’s cover drive to Monty Spanesar’s left-arm spin, the idea is simple: give every dog a recognisable game, then invite people to choose their teammate.

Four small choices behind the pictures

Making the kennel.

Each character gets its own silhouette.
Each cricket action gets its own equipment.

01

Brian finds his own look.

Brian Labra’s revised design uses short, clean-cut fur around his head and neck. Chris Tail keeps his distinct locks. The visual language belongs to each original dog, rather than giving every character the same borrowed hairstyle.

02

Monty’s spin starts on the left.

Monty Spanesar is a black-and-white English springer spaniel, with feathered floppy ears visible below a neatly wrapped navy turban. His bowling study keeps the ball in his own left paw; the breed, expression and cricket mechanics all matter.

03

You can read the role.

A batter has a proper bat, a bowler has a ball, and a wicketkeeper has keeper gloves. Equipment is stowed when the action changes, so a bowling paw can release the ball and a keeper can make a two-glove take.

04

Tea means a proper interval.

Brian’s tea scene includes both a visible drink and something to eat. His bat rests nearby. It is a small detail, but it makes the pavilion feel lived in — and gives the dog something to do between overs.

A picture, a game, a tribute

Original dogs.
Real cricket inspiration.

The first collection includes character portraits, standalone cricket illustrations, shareable artwork and ODI/T20 format studies. Some format studies have a finished portrait; proposed action sequences are identified separately. Native animation work continues.

Cricket achievements are presented as dated, sourced tributes. Each dog is an original fictional character, with no claim about a player’s private personality. No athlete, team or cricket body endorsement is implied.
